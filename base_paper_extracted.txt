1. Introduction
In the digital era, the Internet has become the main channel for the public to obtain information. Social media and 
various news platforms have broken the limitations of information dissemination in time and space, allowing 
information to quickly and widely spread. However, false information also leverages these technological advantages 
to exhibit characteristics of fast dissemination, deep concealment, and wide impact, involving multiple aspects such 
as politics, economy, culture, and public health. This type of information not only misleads the public's judgment, 
but may also be used to seek improper benefits, and even in extreme cases, can cause social chaos. The traditional 
method of detecting fake news relies heavily on manual verification, which is not only time -consuming and

Abstract
Due to the widespread use of the Internet and social networking platforms, false news is spreading faster and faster, and its scope 
of influence continues to expand. Such false information not only easily misleads the public, but may also disrupt social ord er, 
and even pose a threat to political stability, economic development, and public health. To address this challenge, this paper
proposes a machine learning based method for detecting fake news. This method mainly involves preprocessing, feature 
extraction, and optimization of news texts, and constructs a hybrid model that integrates traditional machine learning and sh allow 
deep learning to achieve efficient and accurate identification of fake news. In order to verify the effectiveness of the prop osed 
method, this paper conducted multiple comparative experiments to compare the performance of different models in false news 
detection tasks; Finally, a thorough analysis of the experimental results was conducted. The experimental results show that t he 
hybrid model proposed in this paper achieves an accuracy of 95.3%, which is significantly better than a single machine learni ng 
model, providing strong technical support for the automatic recognition and effective governance of fake news.

2. Literature Review
The research on false news detection based on machine learning is becoming increasingly mature, and scholars at 
home and abroad have conducted a series of studies on data preprocessing, feature extraction, and model 
construction.
Park M and Chai S proposed a detection method based on decision trees and Bayesian classifiers, which analyzed 
Twitter news dissemination data, extracted user metadata, dissemination speed and other features to evaluate news 
credibility, pioneering research in this field [3]. However, the mining of text content features is insufficient, and the 
detection accuracy needs to be improved.
Jain et al. proposed a detection method based on propagation time and structural patterns, which constructs a 
model by analyzing the characteristics of rumor propagation path, speed, etc [4]. It focuses on propagation features 
but ignores text semantic information, making it difficult to deal with complex false news types.
Huang et al. proposed a detection framework that integrates content features and social context features, extracts 
text language features and user communication behavior features to construct a multi feature model, which improves 
detection accuracy [5]. However, feature engineering is complex and feature screening optimization is not perfect.
Abdullah N A S et al. proposed a detection method that combines traditional machine learning with large 
language models. They sorted out the techniques from the perspectives of knowledge, style, propagation, and source, 
pointed out the advantages and disadvantages of large language models, emphasized multimodal analysis and model 
transparency [6].
Sudhakar M and Kaliyamurthie K P proposed a multimodal detection method based on multiple comments and 
triple perspectives, which integrates real and large language models to generate comments, and combines self 
review, peer evaluation, and comprehensive evaluation to construct an anti illusion model. The detection effect is 
excellent, but the model is complex, the training cost is high, and it is difficult to deploy lightweight [7].
Jouhar et al. proposed a detection method based on logistic regression, gradient boosting, and XGBoost. TF -IDF 
was used to extract features, and experiments on the Kaggle dataset showed that the accuracy of logistic regression 
reached 94.72%. However, relying on a single statistical feature makes it difficult to capture deep semantic 
information [8].
Swathi P et al. proposed a hybrid deep learning model that integrates BiLSTM and CNN, using Word2Vec to 
obtain word embedding features and combining attention mechanism to improve semantic extraction ability, with 
excellent detection performance, but high training complexity and hardware requirements [9].
Abualigah et al. proposed a detection method based on stacked ensemble learning, which integrates SVC and 
XGBoost models and uses IndoBERT to preprocess text. The accuracy reached 82%, but the generalization ability is 
insufficient on small sample datasets [10].
Al Alshaqi et al. proposed an adaptive detection framework for unseen events, which improves the model's 
generalization ability through adaptive enhancement and graph contrastive learning [11]. However, the 
computational complexity is high and it is difficult to adapt to real-time detection requirements.
Nadeem et al. proposed a detection method based on LSTM -CNN and BERT -CLS, which utilizes the CLS 
embedding layer of BERT to extract semantic features, and combines LSTM and CNN to extract sequence and local 
features, taking into account both semantic and structural features [12]. However, the model has many parameters

3. Methodolog
3.1. Data Preprocessing
Data preprocessing is a fundamental task in the detection process, aimed at removing noise and unifying text 
formats to provide high -quality input for subsequent tasks. The original text often contains irrelevant information 
such as special symbols, punctuation, stop words, etc., which can interfere with the detection effect. This article 
proposes a preprocessing process consisting of four steps.
3.1.1 Data cleaning
First, remove invalid content such as special symbols, punctuation, numbers, URLs, and blank characters from 
the text. Next, deal with missing information: if the missing proportion of a sample exceeds 30%, delete the sample 
directly; If the missing ratio is within 30%, fill it in using the mean or mode filling method. In addition, duplicate 
samples should also be removed to prevent them from interfering with model training and improve processing 
efficiency.
3.1.2 Text Standardization
In the standardization step, all English letters will be uniformly converted to lowercase, while traditional Chinese 
characters will be converted to simplified. For variant characters and common misspellings, corrections should also 
be made. When Chinese and English are mixed, only the meaningful parts are retained, and irrelevant numbers and 
English content (such as dates, numbers, etc.) are removed. In this way, the text format can be kept consistent and 
the content will be more accurate.
3.1.3 Segmentation Processing
When processing Chinese text, use stutter segmentation tools for segmentation. This tool has high accuracy, fast 
processing speed, and supports custom dictionaries, which can better handle complex sentence structures and rare 
words. For English text, use the NLTK tool to segment words, while removing hyphens and abbreviations, and 
breaking down words into basic morphemes. Finally, convert the processed text into a sequence of lexical elements.
3.1.4 Removing stop words
Combine Chinese and English stop word lists to remove words such as "de", "di", and "de" that appear frequently 
but have low semantic contribution. In addition, considering the characteristics of news texts, some custom stop 
words (such as "news", "report", etc.) will be added to further streamline the text content and improve the 
effectiveness of subsequent feature extraction.
3.2. Feature Extraction
Feature extraction is a crucial step in the entire detection process. This article combines statistical features based 
on TF-IDF with semantic features based on BERT, which ensures extraction efficiency and improves recognition 
accuracy. The specific method is as follows:
3.2.1 TF-IDF Statistical Feature Extraction
TF-IDF is a method of measuring the importance of words in text. The basic idea is that if a word appears 
frequently in the current text but not frequently in the entire corpus, then that word has good discriminative ability. 
The specific calculation formula is as follows:
TF-IDF(t,d)=TF(t,d)×IDF(t) (1)
Among them, TF (t, d) is the frequency of word t in text d (the ratio of the number of word occurrences to the 
total number of words in the text); IDF (t) is the inverse document frequency, calculated as IDF(t) = log
N
Nt+1, where 
N is the total number of documents in the corpus, N t is the number of documents containing the word t, and adding 1 
avoids the denominator being 0.
Construct a corpus vocabulary, calculate the TF -IDF value of each text and convert it into a fixed dimensional 
feature vector; Using ANOVA to screen features, retaining features with high variance and discriminability, and

3.2.2 BERT semantic feature extraction
TF-IDF is difficult to capture deep semantics. In this paper, a lightweight DistilBERT model is used to extract 
semantic features, which retains the core functions of BERT, reduces parameters, and improves efficiency. Convert 
the preprocessed text into an input format acceptable to the model, obtain the final layer CLS token output as the 
semantic feature vector after input, and normalize to ensure consistent feature scales.
3.2.3 Feature Fusion
Using feature concatenation to fuse TF -IDF and BERT features, forming high -dimensional feature vectors that 
balance statistical and semantic information; Using PCA to reduce dimensionality and avoid overfitting, the PCA 
linear transformation formula is as follows:
Y = X × W (2)
Among them, X is the fused high -dimensional feature matrix, W is the linear transformation matrix, and Y is the 
reduced low dimensional feature matrix, which can preserve core information, improve model efficiency and 
generalization ability.
3.3. Model Construction and Training
Build a hybrid model of XGBoost and lightweight CNN, divided into feature mapping layer, feature fusion layer, 
and classification output layer, fully utilizing the fused features to achieve efficient classification. The specific 
structure and training process are as follows:
3.3.1 Model Structure Design
(1) Feature Mapping Layer: The PCA reduced features are separately input into XGBoost and Lightweight CNN. 
XGBoost captures non -linear relationships and processes high -dimensional features, while CNN extracts local 
features through convolution and pooling, complementing each other to improve performance.
(2) Feature fusion layer: Weighted summation is used to fuse the output features of two models. XGBoost 
outputs F1 and CNN outputs F2. The formula for calculating the fused feature F is as follows:
F=α×F1+(1-α)×F2 (3)
Among them, a is the weight coefficient (0<a<1), which was determined to be 0.6 through cross validation, 
taking into account the advantages of both models.
(3) Classification output layer: Fusion feature input fully connected layer integration, using Sigmoid activation 
function to output [0,1] interval results. Results greater than 0.5 are fake news, otherwise they are real news.
3.3.2 Model Training Process
(1) Dataset partitioning: Divided into training and testing sets in an 8:2 ratio, with 10% of the training set used as 
the validation set for hyperparameter tuning and overfitting detection.
(2) Hyperparameter setting: Determine the optimal parameters through grid search and cross validation: XGBoost 
learning rate of 0.1, tree depth of 6, 100 iterations, regularization lambda=0.1; 32 CNN convolution kernels, size 3 × 
3, max pooling, fully connected layer with 128 neurons; Adam optimizer learning rate 5 × 10 -6, batch size 32, 
training epochs 50.
(3) Model training: Using cross entropy loss function, monitor performance through validation set, and stop early 
if the loss does not decrease for 5 consecutive rounds of validation; Set a dropout rate of 0.3 for the fully connected 
layer to prevent overfitting.
(4) Model optimization: Adjust hyperparameters and fusion weights, investigate issues such as vanishing 
gradients and overfitting, and ensure the stability and reliability of the model.

4.1.4 Evaluation indicators
Evaluate performance using four indicators: accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score: (1) Accuracy: The 
proportion of correctly predicted samples to the total sample reflects the overall detection effect; (2) Accuracy: The 
proportion of samples predicted as fake news that are actually fake news, reflecting the accuracy of the prediction; 
(3) Recall rate: the proportion of correctly predicted false news samples, reflecting recognition ability; (4) F1 value: 
the harmonic average of precision and recall, which comprehensively reflects the detection performance.

4.2.4 Comprehensive analysis of model performance
Based on the experimental results on the three datasets, the hybrid model proposed in this paper outperforms the 
comparative model in all evaluation indicators, demonstrating significant advantages. Compared to logistic 
regression, this model introduces both statistical and semantic features, which enables better processing of 
structurally complex texts and enhances detection capabilities. Compared with XGBoost, this model utilizes CNN to 
extract semantic information, which can to some extent compensate for the shortcomings of XGBoost in deep 
semantic understanding. Compared with BiLSTM CNN, this model maintains high detection accuracy while 
training faster and requiring lower hardware resources, making it more feasible for practical deployment. In addition, 
the model has achieved good performance on both Chinese and English datasets, indicating its strong generalization 
ability and promising application prospects.

4.3.1 Model advantages
Firstly, the feature extraction method is relatively comprehensive. This model combines TF -IDF and BERT 
features, taking into account both surface statistical information such as word frequency and deep semantic 
information, enhancing the discriminative ability of the features. Secondly, the detection effect is excellent. On three 
datasets, the accuracy and F1 score of the hybrid model exceeded 94%, indicating its reliable ability to identify fake 
news. Thirdly, it has strong practicality. Due to the introduction of lightweight structures and PCA dimensionality

4.3.2 Insufficient Model
Firstly, the ability to detect short texts is still lacking. When the number of words in a text is less than 50, the 
available features are relatively scarce, making it difficult for the model to extract effective information from it, 
resulting in a decrease in detection accuracy. Secondly, there is a lack of processing capability for multimodal 
information. This model only analyzes text data and does not integrate information from other modalities such as 
images and videos, making it difficult to deal with the diverse forms of fake news in reality. Thirdly, the anti -
interference ability is not strong enough. If fake news contains a large amount of confusing irrelevant information, 
these contents will interfere with the feature extraction process, thereby weakening the detection performance of the 
model.
5. Conclusion
This article proposes a hybrid detection strategy that combines TF -IDF and BERT semantic features to address 
the shortcomings of existing false news detection methods. This strategy uses XGBoost and lightweight CNN as 
modeling tools to sequentially complete data preprocessing, feature extraction, model training, and other steps, 
thereby achieving efficient recognition of fake news. The experiment was conducted on three datasets, and the 
results showed that this method achieved the highest accuracy of 95.3%, significantly better than the three control 
methods, and also demonstrated strong adaptability in different data environments. This work enriches the research 
content in the field of false news detection, and the proposed technical solution has high usability in practical 
applications, which can provide certain technical support for the automatic identification and governance of false 
news.
